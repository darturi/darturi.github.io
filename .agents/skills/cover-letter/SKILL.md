# Cover Letter

Write a persuasive account of what Daniel could contribute to the actual role. Use his work as evidence for that account. Respect the user's language, format, emphasis, and cumulative wording preferences. Do not use em dashes in the letter or accompanying explanation.

## Prepare the evidence

Read [references/candidate-sources.md](references/candidate-sources.md), then the relevant resume and portfolio passages. Previous letters are wording examples, not evidence. Preserve individual contribution, employment dates, publication status, benchmark conditions, and project maturity. User corrections and newly supplied materials take priority. Omit or clarify material conflicts; continue work that does not depend on them.

Open the actual posting. Identify the responsibilities and qualifications that matter to the argument, including any eligibility conditions. Research the employer through official product, careers, or engineering pages as needed to understand that work. Mission and values are context, not required letter content. Open supporting pages; search snippets alone do not establish a claim.

Read supplied application answers and confirmed team preferences. Record, briefly:

- The role need and its source.
- The candidate evidence, its source, and the scope it establishes.
- The contribution that evidence supports, with any missing fact or limit.

Reject a connection based only on shared terminology, similar architecture, or an abstract company value. A transferable skill can be relevant across domains, but explain the work it equips Daniel to do without implying experience he lacks. A task the posting explicitly requests is sufficient grounding; an elaborate product analogy is unnecessary.

Use personal motivations only when Daniel has expressed them. Do not invent admiration, industry preferences, career goals, moral commitments, or autobiographical lessons. Ask one focused question if its answer would materially change the letter, while continuing independent drafting. Otherwise use a factual opening grounded in his work and the opportunity. Treat eligibility, availability, relocation, and work authorization as facts requiring confirmation.

## Stage 1: Draft

Choose the strongest supported case for hiring Daniel. Let that case determine the examples, opening, and paragraph order. Do not require a project count, a fixed paragraph formula, or a different lead project merely for variety.

Give the reader enough evidence to judge the contribution. Keep a technical detail only when it explains the difficulty, Daniel's decision, the result, or an ability this role needs. Remove details that merely describe how a project works or repeat the resume. Publications and metrics earn space on the same basis and must retain their qualifications.

Make the company-specific reason intelligible near the opening. It can concern the actual role, product, or engineering work; it need not express admiration. Develop the hiring argument within the evidence rather than appending a stock relevance sentence to each project summary. When the record supports only a modest connection, state it modestly or omit it.

Use first-person language Daniel could plausibly say to a hiring manager. Prefer actions and clear explanations to abstract traits, lists of virtues, or generic reflections about what an experience taught him. Avoid promotional slogans and inflated claims. Allow the structure to vary with the evidence; valid overlap between applications is not itself a problem.

Normally fit one page, with no minimum word count. Follow explicit application limits and user length preferences. Do not expand to a target by adding a weaker example, an analogy, or a generic reflection. Close with a proportionate contribution or interest in discussing the role.

## Stage 2: Evaluate and revise

Evaluate the completed draft separately from writing it. Review the letter itself, not the persuasiveness of its supporting rationale. Repair can mean replacing or developing a passage, as well as cutting one. If the draft accurately lists qualifications but leaves why they matter here unstated, develop the strongest source-backed connection. Preserve useful employer context when shortening; require a stronger hiring argument, not a shorter letter. Identify passages to change and revise them before delivery:

1. **Support:** Does each factual or autobiographical assertion have a source or user confirmation? Check implied ownership, production scale, publication status, results, and personal motives as well as explicit claims. A prospective contribution is an inference, not an established achievement.
2. **Fit:** Does the evidence demonstrate a responsibility or qualification in this job? Can the connection be explained in one straightforward step? Remove speculative equivalences and causal claims the record cannot sustain.
3. **Selectivity:** Does each project detail help a hiring manager decide? Cut descriptions, technology lists, metrics, and extra examples whose removal leaves the argument intact.
4. **Personalization:** Does the letter show why this candidate fits this opportunity? Naming a product or quoting a mission is insufficient. Check whether the reasoning, example selection, or contribution would need to change for a materially different role. Do not manufacture a personal reason when none is known.
5. **Voice:** Would Daniel plausibly say these sentences aloud? Replace abstract self-praise, polished slogans, repetitive fit claims, and invented lessons with concrete supported language. Plain prose may still sound generic if it makes no useful point.
6. **Structure:** Does each paragraph advance the argument? Consolidate repeated takeaways and vary paragraph purposes where needed. Preserve valid shared evidence across letters; do not invent novelty.
7. **Constraints:** Check requested format and length, cumulative edits, material eligibility gaps, and the absence of em dashes.

Fix substantive failures first, then edit for voice and economy. Do not report a numeric quality score unless requested. Stop when the identified issues are resolved or require missing evidence; omit unsupported content and flag a material unresolved gap briefly.

## Deliver and revise

Default to an editable draft in chat. Export a document only when requested. Keep research citations outside the letter unless requested inside it.

After a full draft, give a short cited rationale for the main choices: the employer need, the verified candidate evidence, and the interpretation connecting them. Cite direct official pages and absolute local file links. The rationale should explain the choices without adding a stronger claim than the letter supports. Flag only material evidence gaps.

Treat feedback as cumulative. For a focused wording edit, preserve accepted passages and return the replacement directly. For a full regeneration, incorporate accepted edits and repeat the separate evaluation stage. Include a cited rationale for a full draft or a new substantive connection.
